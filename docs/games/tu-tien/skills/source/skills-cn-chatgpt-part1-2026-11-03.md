10 cross-cutting rules for terrifying + beautiful xianxia skills

Change the world, not merely the projectile.
A high-tier cast should affect sky, weather, terrain, lighting, gravity, vegetation, surrounding weapons or the metaphysical “domain.” If only the projectile gets larger, it still feels like an ordinary game spell.

Give every school a form of authority.
Sword commands weapons; Metal commands sharpness/hardness; Wood commands life; Water commands domains and flow; Fire commands transformation; Earth commands mass/reality; Thunder commands heavenly judgment; Wind commands weather; Ice commands cessation. The player should feel that nature is obeying, not that they bought a stronger VFX emitter.

Use one gigantic readable idea before adding detail.
“Ten thousand swords forming a celestial wheel,” “one impossible mountain,” “a dragon inside the storm,” “a lotus smaller than a hand,” “a beast under an ocean” all survive distance. Filigree, runes and particles come second. This is particularly important at your 55° camera and 40-px combat silhouettes.

Numbers should become geometry.
72 swords are more impressive when they form rings, gates, constellations, rivers or a killing enclosure. A thousand random particles read as noise; a thousand objects obeying one formation read as cultivation mastery. The Great Geng Sword Formation is the clearest reference. 
bidutuijian.com

Exploit impossible scale contrast.
Tiny lotus → city-level blast. One raindrop → immortal-killing threat. Thin willow branch → defeats giant beings. One spoken command → a mountain's worth of swords answers. Xianxia gets much of its grandeur from making something delicate carry absurd consequences.

F0 can be lethal while the spectacle keeps unfolding.
This is especially important for your typing game. On the final correct key, the target can immediately flash, recoil or take the first hit. The giant dragon, heavenly gate, sword formation or domain can finish revealing itself during F1–F60+. The research does not justify inserting a cinematic delay before damage.

Use sound as a power hierarchy.
Small skills: breath, blade bark, wordless shout. Major skills: brief Mandarin invocation → unnatural reduction in ambience → one unmistakable signature sound → impact. Zhongli's popularity is a concrete example of viewers responding to callout + music + visual event as one synchronized identity. 
TapTap

The strongest skills should create a before/after state.
Before: living beasts, normal sky, intact earth. After: petrified field, frozen corpses, blackened ground, embedded swords, smoking craters, severed vegetation, lingering spirit silhouettes. Without aftermath, even a huge explosion feels disposable.

Let victims sell the cultivator's power.
A 100-beast wave is an advantage, not a VFX problem: dozens bow under wind pressure, a front rank freezes simultaneously, bodies lift before a spear dragon arrives, enemies turn their heads toward swords hunting them from above, elites brace and stagger while trash beasts evaporate. “Power” becomes believable when the population reacts coherently.

An ultimate should contain one moment of impossible calm.
The most intimidating scenes do not remain noisy from beginning to end. A storm can stop for the chant. Ten thousand swords can become perfectly still for six frames. Fire can collapse into one silent lotus. Snow can suspend in the air. Then release everything. That contrast is what makes “khủng bố” feel divine rather than merely busy.

The strongest direction coming out of Part 1 is therefore:

Do not redesign the schools as six bigger attacks each. Redesign each school around a supernatural law that escalates from weapon technique → battlefield authority → mythic phenomenon.
