## What is in them

- **Pyroclast sample:** only **Ember Puff** in the Inferno colour (48 frames at 60 fps, 0.8 s, straight alpha, sheets at 512 and 256 px, loose frames, WebM). It is a small fire poof with sparks and a wisp of smoke, usable as a secondary layer.
- **SCORCH sampler:** 8 effects with 256 px cells, straight-alpha and additive sheets, and `effects.json`:
  - `blast-core` (24 frames, one-shot): a flash ring, then a soft orange blob that turns into dark red-brown smoke over grass;
  - `flashover` (32 frames, one-shot): a rising fire column;
  - `dragon-breath` and `fire-line` (32 frames, looped);
  - `fireball-travel`, `torch-flame` (24, looped);
  - `muzzle-bloom` (12, one-shot);
  - `cinder-drift` (32, additive, looped).

## Coordinator's look (31/10)

- Both packs are generated by code. They read as soft procedural blobs, not painted fire.
- The free parts do not contain a main STRONG explosion at the level the judges asked for:
  - SCORCH's `blast-core` ends in brown smoke over grass, the colour every judge round penalised;
  - Pyroclast's big effects (Fireball Burst, Ground Blast, Shockwave Ring) are paid.
- Possible later uses: `cinder-drift` and Ember Puff as spark layers, and `flashover` or `fire-line` for ground fire.
